5 · AI and training data

Beta Art exists partly because the difference between human and machine authorship has stopped being obvious. Our position is explicit, and it is the same on all three sites.

Reservation of rights

All content on all Beta Art properties is expressly reserved against text and data mining under Article 4(3) of Directive (EU) 2019/790 and the corresponding provision of åndsverkloven. This reservation is machine-readable: it is reinforced by the noai, noimageai directive on every page and by explicit disallow rules for AI crawlers in robots.txt — GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended, CCBot, Bytespider, Applebot-Extended, PerplexityBot, Amazonbot, Diffbot, Omgilibot, FacebookBot and cohere-ai among them.

No licence to use Beta Art material as training, fine-tuning, validation or evaluation data, as retrieval material for a generative system, or as input to synthetic media is granted, offered or implied — including to operators who ignore the reservation. Ignoring it does not create a licence; it creates a claim.

Verified human photography

Every work in the archive is captured by a person with a physical camera. Before a work is accessioned it must pass three tests: the RAW original exists and its checksum is recorded; the capture record and, where available, C2PA content credentials travel with the file; and the photographer signs the licence personally. Generated imagery is never accessioned, and never sold as a plate.

The verification guarantee

A promise with no remedy attached is a slogan, so this one has a remedy. If a plate sold to you as verified is shown to have been generated, composited or materially altered by a machine, then on request:

  • the licence fee for that plate is refunded in full, whenever it was bought and with no time limit;
  • the plate is withdrawn from sale and its catalogue record is marked accordingly; and
  • you keep any right you already have in law — this guarantee is in addition to your statutory rights, never a substitute for them.

The guarantee covers the origin of the photograph. It does not cover ordinary photographic processing — exposure, contrast, colour, cropping, dust removal — which is disclosed on the plate record and is not machine authorship. Noise reduction and sharpening carried out by the camera or the RAW converter are ordinary processing in this sense.

What the human-approved mark covers

The mark on every page says a person has seen what is published and approved it. That is a claim about editorial control, and it is worth being exact about its edges rather than letting it be read as more than it is.

It covers: a person decides what goes up and when. Nothing publishes itself, no page is assembled and released without review, and no image is generated and offered as a photograph. Every photograph, catalogue record, price and legal statement here has been read in English by the person responsible for it.

It does not yet cover: this site is written in twelve languages. The English is written and reviewed by a person. Norwegian, Turkish, Arabic, Chinese, French, German, Hindi, Japanese, Portuguese, Russian and Spanish were written by a machine and have not been read by a native speaker of those languages. That is a fact about this build, not a permanent state: Norwegian is next for a plain-language review, and this paragraph shrinks as each language is signed off.

Where a translation and the English differ, the English text governs. If something in your language reads wrongly or means something the English does not, tell us at hallo@beta-art.com — it is a fault worth fixing and not an inconvenience.

How AI is used in the studio

  • A person is responsible for every delivery and approves it before it leaves the studio.
  • AI is used for drafting, structuring, research and repetitive processing — never as a substitute for judgement, and never presented as a photographic original.
  • We will tell you, on request, which parts of your delivery were machine-assisted.
  • Your project material is never submitted to a model that trains on it. Where a service must be used, the training-excluded or enterprise mode is used.

Invoices and proformas

The archive can generate a proforma invoice from your quote basket. Every final invoice carries a sequential number, issue date, due date, itemised scope, net amount, VAT rate and amount, gross total, payment details and the applicable terms. Invoices are issued as PDF by email. For Norwegian public-sector customers we invoice on EHF 3.0 — the Peppol-based national format — which is mandatory for suppliers to the Norwegian state, counties and municipalities, not an option we offer. Accounting records are retained for five years as required by the Norwegian Bookkeeping Act.

Mandatory e-invoicing from 2027

The Norwegian Ministry of Finance has brought forward mandatory B2B e-invoicing to 1 January 2027, when every business subject to Norwegian bookkeeping obligations must issue invoices in a structured electronic format. Mandatory digital bookkeeping and mandatory receipt of e-invoices follow from 1 January 2030. Businesses under kr 50 000 are proposed to be exempt, and the detailed technical rules are expected during 2026.

Beta Art is not yet registered as a business, so it issues no invoices at all today and holds no organisation number or Peppol address. EHF 3.0 is how it will invoice business and public-sector customers from the first one, which is a decision taken early rather than a capability already in use. We raise the date because it changes something for most of our business customers, not because it changes anything for us yet.

Late payment

Interest on overdue amounts accrues at the rate set under the Late Payment Interest Act (forsinkelsesrenteloven), plus the statutory collection fee. Work may be paused, and access to non-transferred accounts withheld, while an invoice is overdue. Rights in deliverables and licences transfer on full payment, not before.

10 · Accessibility

The legal minimum for a private business in Norway is WCAG 2.0 level A and AA, under likestillings- og diskrimineringsloven §18 and forskrift om universell utforming av IKT. Norwegian public bodies must meet WCAG 2.1 A and AA — 48 of the 78 success criteria — in force since 1 February 2023 under the EU Web Accessibility Directive.

Beta Art builds to WCAG 2.2 level AA, which is above both. We do that because the higher standard is what makes a site usable, not because we are obliged to. Supervision is by Tilsynet for universell utforming av IKT, which can order corrections and impose a coercive fine.

The European Accessibility Act (Directive (EU) 2019/882) has applied in the EU since 28 June 2025, but it has not yet been implemented in Norway — transposition in the EEA states is delayed. We build to it anyway, and we will not claim it binds us until it does.

  • Every page works with the keyboard alone, with visible focus indicators.
  • Semantic headings, landmarks, labelled form fields and live regions for status messages.
  • Text contrast meets or exceeds 4.5:1; interface contrast 3:1.
  • Motion is removed entirely when the operating system requests reduced motion.
  • No information is carried by colour alone; nothing depends on hover.
  • Text resizes to 200% without loss of content or function.
  • Every photographic plate carries a real alternative text describing what is in the frame.

If any part of these sites blocks you, write to hallo@beta-art.com. We treat it as a defect, fix it, and give you the information another way in the meantime.

11 · Enforcement

Rights that are never enforced stop being rights. Beta Art enforces.

  • Unlicensed use of a photographic work, a text, the mark or the page design is invoiced at three times the standard licence fee for the equivalent use, plus the cost of investigation — the established measure for unauthorised use in Nordic practice.
  • Watermark removal, metadata stripping or circumvention of the preview restriction is treated as a separate infringement of rights-management information under åndsverkloven and the WIPO Copyright Treaty.
  • Takedowns are issued to the host, the registrar, the CDN, the platform and the search engine simultaneously — under the DMCA in the United States, the DSA in the EU, and the equivalent notice procedure elsewhere.
  • Cloned sites and copied concepts are pursued as copyright infringement of the design and copy and, where the mark or get-up is involved, as trade-mark infringement and unfair competition under markedsføringsloven §§25 and 30.
  • Evidence is preserved routinely: dated source archives, RAW originals with checksums, C2PA content credentials on published imagery, and timestamped deployment records.
  • Jurisdiction: Norwegian law, Stavanger tingrett — without prejudice to a consumer's right to their home forum, or to our right to seek injunctive relief wherever the infringement occurs.

If you believe your own rights have been affected by something published here, write to hallo@beta-art.com. We answer every notice, and we remove anything that should not be up.

13 · Reporting something unlawful

If you believe something published on a Beta Art property is unlawful — it infringes your copyright, it is a photograph of you that you did not consent to, it is defamatory, or it breaks another law — tell us and we will act.

Report it here: the notice form, or by email to hallo@beta-art.com with the word NOTICE in the subject line.

What to include

These four things, which are what Article 16 of the Digital Services Act (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065) asks a notice to contain:

  • The exact address of the content — the page URL, and the plate number if there is one
  • An explanation of why you believe it is unlawful, specific enough to be checked
  • Your name and an email address, so we can reply. You may omit these if the report concerns an offence under Articles 3 to 7 of Directive 2011/93/EU.
  • A statement that you believe, in good faith, that the information is accurate and complete

What we do

  • Acknowledge the notice by email, without undue delay.
  • Decide in a timely, diligent, non-arbitrary way, and tell you the outcome with the reasons for it.
  • Act where the notice is well founded: the content comes down, and where it is a photograph, the plate is withdrawn from sale and any live licence is refunded.
  • Tell the other side. If the content came from someone else, they are told what was decided and why, and can dispute it.

No automated system makes these decisions. A person reads every notice, which is slower and is the only way an answer can carry a reason.

If you disagree with the outcome

Reply to the decision and say why; it will be reconsidered. You may also take the matter to an out-of-court dispute settlement body, or to a court. Nothing here limits that, and nothing here is a condition of it.

Withdrawing consent to your own image

What has already been licensed to a buyer cannot always be recalled — we will tell you exactly what is out and what we have done about it, rather than promise more than we can deliver.
