# HXI — a business developer's read

Written August 2026, from the verified figures in `docs/RESEARCH.md`. Where a number is the
artist's own rather than independently checked, it says so.

## What this is right now

**An artist with an audience, not yet a business with revenue.** The distinction matters
because almost every decision below follows from it.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly listeners | 251,000 |
| Spotify followers | 19,834 — a **7.9%** follow rate against an industry 15–25% |
| Catalogue | ~25 releases, 2022–2026 |
| Owned contacts | **0** |
| Products for sale | **0** |
| Revenue lines under HXI's control | **0** |

Every one of those 251,000 people is rented. Spotify knows who they are; HXI does not.

## The number that defines the risk

**"help urself" is roughly 84–88% of catalogue streams.** One track, released December 2022,
carries the artist. The range is the honest form of the figure: HXI's own documents put it at
~88% in the earlier Competitive Brief and ~84% in everything more recent, and the denominator
in both is "known streams" — an incomplete base, since there are no verified per-track counts
for the whole catalogue. Nothing in the argument below depends on which end of the range is
right; at 84% the risk is the same shape as at 88%.

That is not a growth curve, it is an annuity — and streaming annuities decay. The peer table
in the brand pack (LXNGVX 386K, Slowboy 299K, HXI 251K, D4C 185K) reads like a competitive
position, but it measures monthly listeners, and HXI's are largely one 2022 track still being
used in edits. The gap between him and the artist above him is not the point; the gap between
his top track and his second one is. That ratio is roughly 1,000:1.

**Two readings, and both are true.** Pessimistically: the business is a decaying single asset.
Optimistically: the catalogue has never been monetised at all, so every number below is
upside from a base of zero.

## The asset nobody has priced

43 million streams arrived with **no promotion budget**. They came from editors who needed
music they could use.

That is a distribution channel most artists pay for and cannot buy, and it is currently earning
nothing beyond streaming royalties. It is also the only channel here with a proven conversion
rate, because it already converted once at scale.

Everything in the store that targets that community — stems, the drum kit, the presets, the
edit pack — is an attempt to price a relationship that already exists. That is a far better
bet than a hoodie, which asks a stranger to become a walking advertisement.

## Revenue lines, honestly sized

**Streaming.** Exists, declining, uncontrollable. Lifetime gross on the hit is on the order of
$130–215K at $0.003–0.005 per stream — an estimate from the artist's own pack, before the
label, collaborators and distributor take their share, and spread across four years. It is not
an annual figure and should never be treated as one.

**Sync — the real business.** Fast & Furious and two BODYCAM tracks are not luck; they are a
demonstrated capability, and mid-tier placements run in the thousands per track against
fractions of a cent per stream. It is lumpy and relationship-driven, which is exactly why it
is undervalued by artists who prefer channels with dashboards. **Highest expected value per
hour invested of anything on this page.**

**Digital products.** Best margin in the catalogue, zero marginal cost, small ticket. Be
sober about the arithmetic: 0.5% of 251,000 listeners buying one 149 NOK pack is roughly
187,000 NOK gross — and 0.5% is optimistic for an audience that has never been asked for
anything. Treat the first product as a measurement, not a revenue plan.

**Merch.** Real money, 50–70% gross margin, and the most operationally expensive line here.
Sizes, returns, customs, support. For one person it competes directly with making music.

**Membership.** The only recurring line, and the only one whose constraint is not demand but
**HXI's time**: something has to ship every month, forever. Do not launch it until three
months of content exist unshipped.

## The structural problem

**One person is the label, the studio, the shop and the support desk.** Every line above adds
operational load to the person who also has to make the next record — and the next record is
the only thing that actually grows the business.

The store now has twelve products. Launching twelve is the wrong move: twelve times the
support surface to learn one funnel. **Ship one, learn, then widen.**

## Unresolved legal exposure, in order of expected cost

1. **"HXI" is not trademarked.** Procedure, fees and the order to do it in are in
   `docs/TRADEMARK.md`. The brand pack says so plainly. Merch is about to be sold
   under a name nobody has secured — and the moment it sells, the name is worth filing against.
   Norway first (Patentstyret), then EUIPO. This is the cheapest high-value action available
   and it is not on anyone's list.
2. **The GramArt confirmation is verbal.** The entire "independent, owns his masters"
   positioning — including a line on the live page — rests on an unwritten phone call. Get it
   in writing before the page carries it much longer.
3. **The Scar Scheme contract was signed as "HXI"** with master and name ownership recorded as
   unresolved in the artist's own document. Until that is read by a lawyer, points 1 and 2 are
   both partly unanswerable.

None of this blocks shipping a sample pack. All of it blocks scaling.

## What I would do, in this order

1. **File the trademark.** Weeks of lead time, small money, and everything else assumes it.
2. **Get GramArt in writing.** One email that already exists as a draft in the archive.
3. **Ship one digital product** — the drum kit. Lowest ops, files already exist, and it tells
   you whether this audience buys anything at all. That answer changes every later decision.
4. **Build the list.** 251,000 listeners and zero owned contacts is the single largest gap in
   this business. A small list converting at 2.5% earns what tens of thousands of streams earn.
5. **Write the sync one-pager.** Highest value per hour, and the credits to justify it already
   exist.
6. **Merch last — or license it.** A partner running merch under licence converts an
   operations problem into a royalty. For a one-person operation that is usually the right
   trade.

## What not to do

- Launch twelve products at once.
- Buy ads to a homepage.
- Chase a second viral moment. It is not a strategy; it is weather. Build the things that
  capture it when it comes — which, as of this week, the page finally does.
