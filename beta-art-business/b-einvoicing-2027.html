Every Norwegian business must issue e-invoices from 1 January 2027

The date moved forward a year and most small companies have not heard. Here is what actually changes, and what to do about it in 2026 rather than in December.

Beta Art · 2026-08-18 · 6 min read

What was decided

The Ministry of Finance has brought mandatory business-to-business e-invoicing forward by a year, from 1 January 2028 to 1 January 2027. From that date every business subject to Norwegian bookkeeping obligations must issue invoices in a structured electronic format — not a PDF, not a scan, not an attachment.

A second stage follows on 1 January 2030: mandatory digital bookkeeping, and a mandatory ability to receive e-invoices. Businesses under kr 50 000 in turnover are proposed to be exempt. The detailed technical rules are expected during 2026.

What counts as an e-invoice

Not a PDF. This is the single most common misunderstanding, and it will cost people time in December 2026. A PDF is a picture of an invoice; the requirement is for structured data a machine can read without guessing — in practice EHF, Norway’s Peppol-based national format, already mandatory for anyone selling to the state, counties or municipalities.

If you already invoice a kommune, you are already doing this. If you have never sold to the public sector, you probably are not.

Who this affects most

  • Sole proprietorships above kr 50 000 who invoice from a spreadsheet or a Word template. This is the group with the most work to do and the least warning.
  • Small companies on an old accounting package that exports PDF only. Check whether your version supports EHF, not whether the vendor’s website mentions it.
  • Anyone invoicing through a webshop or booking system that generates its own documents. That system now has a compliance requirement attached to it.

What to do in 2026, in order

  1. Find out what your current system can do. One question to your accounting provider: does this issue EHF 3.0 over Peppol, on my plan? Get the answer in writing.
  2. If the answer is no, change provider this year, not next. Migrating an accounting system in the same quarter as the deadline is how people end up invoicing late.
  3. Get a Peppol address. Your provider usually registers it for you; it takes days, not months.
  4. Send one real invoice through it to a customer who can confirm receipt. A test invoice that nobody opens proves nothing.
  5. Check your customer data. Organisation numbers must be correct and present. This is the boring failure that stops delivery, and it is easier to fix now than at volume.

The part worth saying plainly

This is not a reason to buy anything from us. For most small companies it is a conversation with the accounting provider you already pay, and it ends there. If your invoicing runs through a system somebody built for you — a shop, a booking flow, a custom back office — then it is a development task, and that is where we would be useful.

Either way, the mistake is treating a 1 January date as a January problem. The systems people are queuing for in December will be the same systems, with a queue.
