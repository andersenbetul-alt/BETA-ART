AI in a small business: what works, what wastes money

After a year of building these for other people, a short and unglamorous list.

Beta Art · 2026-07-22 · 7 min read

What reliably works

  • First drafts. Of anything you would otherwise stare at: quotes, replies, product descriptions, job adverts. The edit is faster than the blank page.
  • Sorting and extracting. Pulling structured fields out of unstructured email. Deeply boring, immediately valuable.
  • Answering from your own documents. A support assistant that quotes your own manual is useful. One that improvises is a liability.
  • Research summaries where you will check the sources anyway.

What wastes money

  • Buying a tool before naming a task. The licence is the cheap part; the setup nobody did is the expensive part.
  • Publishing unedited output. It is recognisable, and it costs you the credibility the content was meant to build.
  • Chatbots with no escalation. A bot that cannot say “I will get a person” is worse than no bot.
  • Replacing judgement. Pricing, hiring, anything legal. The confident wrong answer is the dangerous one.

The question to ask a supplier

Ask what happens to your data. Specifically: does the material we send train a model, and which service is it going to? If the answer is vague, that is the answer. The right answer names the service and says training-excluded or enterprise mode, in writing.

Ask the second question too: which parts of what you deliver are machine-assisted? A supplier who will not answer that is charging you for judgement they are not applying.

A realistic first year

Automate one task. Live with it for a month. Then automate the next. Companies that buy a platform and try to move everything at once usually end up back where they started, plus a subscription.

The businesses getting real value from this are not doing anything exotic. They picked the dullest repeated task in the week, handed it over properly, wrote down how it works, and moved on to the next one. That is the whole method.
