The CV format that clears applicant-tracking systems

Sixty applications and four replies is usually not a talent problem. It is a parsing problem, and it is fixable in an afternoon.

Beta Art · 2026-08-05 · 6 min read

What actually happens to your file

Before a person sees your CV, software reads it. It extracts text, looks for the terms in the advert, and ranks the result. It reads top to bottom, left to right, and it does not understand design.

Which means a two-column layout with your job titles in a sidebar can parse as an empty document. The information is on the page; it is not in the file in an order the parser can follow. The candidate never knows, because the rejection is silent.

The rules that matter

  • One column. Sidebars, text boxes and tables are where information goes to disappear.
  • Real headings. The words Experience, Education, Skills — not clever alternatives. The parser is looking for the boring words.
  • Dates in one format, on the same line as the role. Month Year – Month Year.
  • No text inside images. A logo is fine. Your name as a graphic is not.
  • .docx or a text-based PDF. A PDF exported from a design tool is often one flat image with no text at all.
  • File name: firstname-lastname-cv.pdf. It is the first thing a human reads.

What to write, once it can be read

Lead with the result, not the responsibility. “Responsible for social media” tells a reader nothing. “Grew the account from 400 to 6 000 followers in nine months, and the enquiries that came with it” tells them what happens if they hire you.

Put a number in at least half your lines. If you have no numbers, use scale: how many people, how many customers, how big a budget. Never invent one — you will be asked about it in the interview, and that is a worse outcome than a vague line.

Tailoring without rewriting

You do not need a new CV per application. You need three variable lines: the summary sentence, the top three skills, and the first bullet of your most recent role. Change those three to echo the advert’s own words. Leave everything else alone.

That takes four minutes per application instead of forty, which matters, because volume is part of the job search whether anyone admits it or not.

The uncomfortable part

None of this makes a weak application strong. It makes a strong application readable. If you rebuild the format and still get no replies after twenty applications, the problem is upstream — the roles, the market, or the story you are telling about the change you are making. That is a different conversation, and it is worth having honestly rather than sending the twenty-first.
