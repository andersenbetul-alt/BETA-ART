What a website actually costs in Norway, and why nobody will tell you

Three quotes for the same brief came back at kr 18 000, kr 140 000 and “let’s have a meeting”. All three were honest. Here is what moves the number.

Beta Art · 2026-08-12 · 7 min read

The number depends on four things, and only four

Every quote you receive is a function of scope, content, integrations and who maintains it afterwards. Agencies rarely say this out loud, because saying it invites you to reduce the scope — which reduces the invoice.

Scope. A one-page site and a forty-page site are not the same product. If you sell one thing, one page will out-perform five, and it costs a fifth as much. Most companies buy pages they will never update because the sitemap was drawn before anyone asked what each page was for.

Content. Who writes it? This is the single biggest hidden cost. A build quoted at kr 24 000 assumes the text exists. It usually does not, and the project then stalls for two months while somebody who is not a writer tries to write. Writing the content is real work and should be a line on the quote, not an assumption.

Integrations. Booking, payment, CRM, inventory, a login. Each one is a system that can break independently and needs testing against real data. Two integrations can cost more than the entire front end.

Maintenance. Who edits it in March? If the answer is “the agency”, you are buying a subscription, not a website. That is a legitimate choice — but it should be priced and named, not discovered later.

Realistic ranges, in NOK excluding VAT

  • kr 9 000 – 15 000 — one page, your content, no integrations. A campaign page or a personal site.
  • kr 24 000 – 40 000 — a real company site, five to twelve pages, content written for you, no complex integrations.
  • kr 40 000 – 90 000 — the above plus e-commerce or two or three integrations, or several languages.
  • Above kr 100 000 — custom systems, many languages, or a team of five people, which is a different kind of supplier from us.

Why the cheap quote is sometimes right

A kr 18 000 quote is not automatically a warning sign. It is the correct price for a small scope done by someone efficient. It becomes a problem when it is the correct price for a small scope and you were describing a large one — and neither party noticed, because nobody wrote the scope down.

That is the actual test. Not the number: whether the quote comes with a written scope you can hold it to. A cheap quote with a precise scope is safe. An expensive quote with a vague one is not.

The four questions to ask every supplier

  1. Who writes the content, and is it in this price?
  2. What happens if we are late — for you, and for us?
  3. Who owns the source files, the domain and the hosting account after launch?
  4. What does a small change cost in six months, and who does it?

Any supplier who answers those four clearly is worth talking to at any price. Any supplier who deflects them is expensive at every price.
