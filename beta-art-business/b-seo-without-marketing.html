SEO for a company with no marketing department

Four things, in order. Do them and you will beat most of your competitors, who have not.

Beta Art · 2026-07-08 · 7 min read

One: fix what is broken before writing anything

Content on a broken site is money set on fire. Check four things: every page has one h1 that describes it, every page has a unique title under sixty characters, the site loads in under two and a half seconds on a phone, and Search Console is verified so you can see what is happening.

That is usually a day of work, and it is the highest-return day you will spend.

Two: one page per thing you sell

The single most common structural mistake is a services page listing eight services. It ranks for none of them, because it is specifically about nothing. Eight pages, each about one service, each answering that service’s own question, will each rank for their own term.

This is also why every service on this site has its own page. It is not a design preference; it is the structure search rewards, and it happens to be the structure buyers prefer too.

Three: answer the questions you already get asked

Your salespeople answer the same questions every week. Those are the questions people type into search. Write the answers down, publicly, once each. You do not need a content strategy for this — you need a list of the last twenty questions a customer asked, and the discipline to answer one a week.

Four: measure three things, not thirty

Organic sessions, enquiries from organic, and which pages produce them. Everything else is decoration until those three are moving.

What to ignore

  • Keyword density. Not a thing for roughly a decade.
  • Buying links. A risk you cannot unwind.
  • Anyone guaranteeing position one. Nobody can. The guarantee is the tell.
  • Blogging weekly about nothing. Twelve pieces that answer real questions beat fifty that answer none.

How long it takes

Technical fixes show within weeks. Structure shows in one to three months. Content ranks in three to six. Anyone promising faster is describing paid advertising, which is a fine thing to buy — but it stops the day you stop paying, and this does not.
