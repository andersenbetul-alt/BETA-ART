Commercial — price on request

No licence issued by Beta Art permits use of any image, in whole or in part, as training, fine-tuning, validation or evaluation data for generative or predictive machine-learning systems. This exclusion survives termination of the licence and applies to every tier, including Extended and Custom.

Automated collection of images or text from this site for dataset construction is prohibited. Beta Art declares this reservation of rights by every means available to it, including the robots directives published at /robots.txt, the noai and noimageai declarations in the page head, and this notice.

Where a camera supports it, C2PA Content Credentials are written at capture, so a delivered file can be shown to originate from a physical camera rather than a model.

5 · Payment and invoicing

Licensing runs on documents, not on impulse purchases. The sequence is:

  1. Quote. You select plates and receive a proforma document listing each plate, its accession record and the licence price. Nothing is payable at this stage.
  2. Licence. The licence is drafted for the agreed media, term and territory and countersigned by the photographer.
  3. Invoice. A Norwegian invoice is issued by email, stating the organisation number and VAT where applicable. Payment terms are 14 days net unless agreed otherwise.
  4. Delivery. On payment you receive a download link valid for 72 hours, the full-resolution file (sRGB JPEG, or TIFF on request) and a numbered PDF licence certificate.

Accepted payment methods: card, Vipps and bank transfer. Prices on this site are shown in Norwegian kroner and exclude VAT unless stated. If a download link expires, write to hallo@beta-art.com with the order number and a fresh link will be issued.

6 · Refunds and withdrawal

Because photographs are delivered digitally and the file remains with you, refunds are limited to technical delivery failures and duplicate charges.

Under Norwegian law consumers have a 14-day right to withdraw from a digital purchase — unless you consent to immediate delivery, in which case that right is waived. You are asked to confirm this explicitly before the file is released. Business purchases are not covered by the consumer withdrawal right.

7 · Privacy

This site sets no advertising cookies and runs no third-party analytics. The only data stored in your browser is what you put there yourself: your chosen language, your reading preference, and any plates you add to a quote. All of it stays on your device and can be cleared at any time.

When you send a licence request or a commission brief, the details you provide are used to answer that request, to draft a licence and to issue an invoice, and are kept for as long as Norwegian bookkeeping rules require. They are never sold or passed to advertisers.

People photographed for the archive appear with a signed model release. If you appear in a plate and want it withdrawn, write to hallo@beta-art.com and it will be removed from the public catalogue.

7b · Global privacy compliance

Beta Art is established in Norway and processes personal data under the EEA regime, but the site is reachable worldwide. Where another country's law gives a visitor stronger rights, those rights are honoured rather than argued about.

Which laws are applied

  • EU / EEA — GDPR (Regulation 2016/679), as implemented in Norway by the personopplysningsloven; supervisory authority: Datatilsynet.
  • United Kingdom — UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018; supervisory authority: ICO.
  • Switzerland — revised FADP (nFADP, in force September 2023).
  • Türkiye — KVKK (Law 6698 on the Protection of Personal Data).
  • California — CCPA as amended by CPRA, including the right to opt out of sale or sharing. Beta Art does not sell or share personal information, so there is nothing to opt out of.
  • Brazil — LGPD (Law 13.709/2018).
  • Canada — PIPEDA, and Quebec's Law 25.
  • South Africa — POPIA.
  • Singapore — PDPA; Japan — APPI; South Korea — PIPA.
  • China — PIPL, for visitors located in mainland China.
  • India — DPDP Act 2023.
  • Australia — Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles.

What is collected, and why

  • Nothing by default. No advertising cookies, no third-party analytics, no tracking pixels, no fingerprinting. Fonts are the only external request the pages make.
  • Local storage on your own device — your language choice, your reading mode, your quote basket and whether you dismissed the notice. This never leaves your browser and is not personal data in our hands.
  • What you send us — name, email, organisation, and what you write in a licence request or brief. Legal basis: performance of a contract or steps taken at your request (GDPR Art. 6(1)(b)), and our legitimate interest in answering enquiries (Art. 6(1)(f)).
  • Invoicing data — kept because Norwegian bookkeeping law requires it (Art. 6(1)(c)), currently five years after the financial year in question.

Your rights, wherever you are

  • Access — a copy of what is held about you.
  • Rectification — correction of anything wrong.
  • Erasure — deletion, except where bookkeeping law requires retention.
  • Restriction and objection — including objection to any processing based on legitimate interest.
  • Portability — your data in a machine-readable format.
  • Withdrawal of consent — at any time, without affecting what happened before.
  • No automated decision-making — nothing here profiles you or decides anything about you automatically.
  • Non-discrimination — exercising a right never changes the price or the service you receive.

To exercise any of these, write to hallo@beta-art.com. Requests are answered within 30 days. If you are unhappy with the answer you may complain to your own supervisory authority — in Norway that is Datatilsynet.

Transfers, processors and security

Data stays within the EEA wherever possible. Where a service provider is outside it, transfers rely on an adequacy decision or on the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses with a transfer impact assessment on file. Processors are limited to hosting, email and payment providers, each under a written data-processing agreement. Access is restricted to the photographer and, where a project requires it, named collaborators under confidentiality.

People in photographs

Anyone identifiable in a plate appears with a signed release. Under GDPR a photograph of an identifiable person is personal data: if you appear in one and want it withdrawn, write to us and it is removed from the public catalogue, and from onward licensing, without argument.

Children

This site is not directed at children, and we do not knowingly collect data from anyone under 16 (or the age set by local law). Children appear in photographs only with documented consent from a parent or guardian.

8 · Enforcement

Unlicensed use is pursued. Every plate is catalogued, checksummed and dated, which makes both the fact of ownership and the date of first publication straightforward to evidence.

  • Unlicensed commercial use is invoiced at three times the applicable licence fee, without prejudice to any further claim.
  • Infringing pages are reported to the host, the registrar and the relevant search engines under the applicable notice-and-takedown procedure.
  • Disputes are governed by Norwegian law, with Stavanger tingrett as the agreed venue.

To report misuse of a Beta Art image, or to ask whether a use is licensed, write to hallo@beta-art.com.

9 · Registrations in progress

Copyright needs no registration: it exists from creation, and this page is part of the dated public record of it. Trade marks and design rights do require formal application, and those applications are a separate, chargeable legal process.

The steps prepared for Beta Art, in the order they are normally taken:

  1. Trade mark — Norway. Word mark "BETA ART" and the aperture-and-seal figurative mark, filed with Patentstyret in classes 16, 35, 41 and 42.
  2. Trade mark — EU. Corresponding EUTM application at EUIPO, claiming priority from the Norwegian filing within six months.
  3. International extension. WIPO Madrid Protocol designation for further markets once the base registration is granted.
  4. Registered design. The accession label layout and the aperture-and-seal mark as a registered design, where local rules allow.
  5. Domain and handle protection. Defensive registration of the principal top-level domains and social handles.

Status: prepared, not yet filed. Nothing on this page should be read as a claim that a trade mark or design is already registered. Beta Art will publish registration numbers here once they are granted, and no ™ or ® symbol is used on this site until then.
