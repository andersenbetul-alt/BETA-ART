What to add

Three values, because one hue cannot do all three jobs: a fill that reads at small sizes, an ink dark enough for text, and a tint for quiet backgrounds.

TokenValueOn #faf7eeUse for
--accent-warm #e9915e 2.4:1 — never text Shapes and fills: the mark's figure, button grounds, icon strokes
--accent-warm-ink #a84a18 5.3:1 — passes AA Text on the warm tint or on paper: eyebrows, small labels, numbers
--accent-warm-soft #fbe6d7 ground only Chips, step-number tiles, the quiet background behind warm ink 
/* app/globals.css — alongside the existing tokens */
:root {
  --accent-warm:      #e9915e;
  --accent-warm-ink:  #a84a18;
  --accent-warm-soft: #fbe6d7;
}

Where it goes, and where it doesn't

The mark

The figure held by the cradle. It is the one element that must read at 20 px, and green-on-green does not.

The one action per screen

Whichever button you actually want pressed. If two buttons are warm, neither is.

Progress and sequence

Step numbers, the eyebrow above a headline — small marks that say "you are moving forward".

Not these

Body text, links inside paragraphs, borders, card grounds, verification badges, or anything expressing safety. Trust is the green's job; warmth is the accent's. Keep it under a tenth of any screen.

The palette values here were read from the live stylesheet at naviar-care-1.vercel.app, so the left-hand panes match what ships today. Contrast ratios are computed against the product's own #faf7ee ground, not against white.
