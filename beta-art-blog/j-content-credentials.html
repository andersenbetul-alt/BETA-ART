Essay · Provenance

Content credentials, switched on from the first frame

Signing a file after the doubt arrives proves that you signed a file. It does not prove where the file came from.

By the archivist · 2 April 2026 · 5 min read

Content credentials are a signed record travelling with an image: what device made it, what was done to it afterwards, and whether any step was generative. The specification behind them, C2PA, is an industry effort with camera makers, editing-software makers and news organisations behind it.

The useful part is not the technology. It is the timing.

Why in camera, and not later

A credential attached at export says the file existed when you exported it. A credential written at capture says a specific device produced this image at a specific moment, and that everything since is recorded as an edit rather than an origin.

The difference only shows up under challenge, which is exactly when it matters. Anyone can sign anything at any point. The question a buyer's legal team asks is not whether the file is signed — it is what the signature was attached to, and when.

A signature applied after the question is asked answers a different question.

What the support actually looks like

Honest summary: partial, improving, and not yet a plan you can rely on alone.

  • Cameras. A small number of bodies can sign at capture, some through firmware added after release. Check your specific body and firmware version rather than the manufacturer's press page.
  • Editing software. Broader support for recording edits, though the credential survives some export paths and not others.
  • Platforms. Uneven. Several strip metadata on upload, which is why the credential is the archive's record and not the buyer's proof.
  • Everything else. Phones, older bodies, scanners: nothing. Which is most working equipment, including much of the equipment this archive uses.

So what carries the weight in the meantime

The same three records that carried it before any of this existed: the RAW original with a checksum, filed within a day; the capture record written at the shoot; and a release signed by anyone recognisable in the frame.

Content credentials are an addition to that, not a replacement for it. An archive that depends on a credential is one platform update away from having no evidence at all, and the checksum on a RAW file has never needed anyone else's cooperation.

The practical setting

If your body supports it, turn it on today and leave it on, including for the frames you are certain nobody will ever question. Provenance is only convincing when it is indiscriminate. A photographer who signs selectively has told everyone that the unsigned frames are the interesting ones.

And record honestly where a frame was signed after the fact. In this archive that distinction is a field on the record, not a rounding error — because a record that quietly flatters itself is worth less than no record.
