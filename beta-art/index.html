Verification

Three tests. A work enters the archive only if it passes all of them.

This is the standard the archive is built on. A technically good photograph that fails one test is kept for personal use — it is not catalogued and it is not sold.

  1. Test 01

    Provenance

    Can we state the exact time, place, camera and subject? Coordinates and capture time are written at the shoot and carried on the accession label.

    RAW original archived within 24 hours · filename convention 2026-08-14_bergen_fisker_a1.dng

  2. Test 02

    Not reproducible by a machine

    Is this an identifiable person, a specific place, a particular light — something a generator cannot invent? Generic beauty fails this test.

    Content Credentials (C2PA) enabled in camera from day one · no retroactive signing

  3. Test 03

    A named buyer

    Can we name at least one buyer who would pay kr 890 or more for this frame? If not, the work has no place in a licensing archive.

    Model release signed within 7 days · catalogue decision within 14 days

The photographer

Shot for licensing, not for stock

The working principle behind every plate: provenance before composition. A photograph that cannot say where and when it was made is a nice picture, not an archival record.

That means slower work. Two or three days aboard a boat before the first frame. A workshop visited often enough that the door is simply open. Thirty to a hundred frames per subject, not one lucky shot.

  • Full-frame capture at 60 MP or above, two bodies, no single point of failure
  • RAW original archived within 24 hours, backed up to two locations
  • Model release signed within seven days, catalogue decision within fourteen
  • Content Credentials written in camera — never signed retroactively

FAQ

Questions we are asked most

What makes a work "verified"?

It has passed all three tests — provenance, not-reproducible-by-a-machine, and a named buyer — and it carries a RAW original, a capture record and a signed licence. A work missing any of those is not listed.

Do you license generated images?

No. The archive exists to be the opposite of that, and the licence says so in writing.

May the images be used to train a model?

No. Every licence excludes use as training data. That exclusion is one of the reasons buyers come here in the first place.

Can I get the RAW file?

The RAW original stays in the archive. Extended and custom licences include a RAW-derived master at full capture resolution, which covers production needs.

Why are some locations withheld?

Some sites are fragile, privately owned, or would suffer from the traffic. The licence holder receives the coordinates; the public catalogue does not.

Are people in the photographs paid models?

No. They are people doing their own work, photographed with a signed release. Nothing in the archive is staged.
