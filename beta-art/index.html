Verified Human
Photography.

Beta Art is an archive of original photographs made by people with physical cameras. The archive is designed around provenance: the connection between an image, its original capture, the photographer who made it and the licence granted to the customer.

For every photograph that receives verified status, the original RAW file is preserved and the available capture record is kept with the catalogue entry. Customers can ask to inspect provenance information before licensing an image.

Each plate carries a catalogue number, a capture record and a written licence signed by the photographer. There are no intermediaries, no anonymous uploads and no synthetic images here.

Verification

How each work is verified.

  1. Method I

    The RAW original is archived.

    Every plate begins as a RAW file from a real camera. The originals are stored in an offline archive, available for inspection on request.

  2. Method II

    The capture record travels with the image.

    Camera, lens, exposure, place and date accompany each photograph. Where the equipment supports it, C2PA Content Credentials are embedded at capture.

  3. Method III

    The licence is signed by the maker.

    You license directly from the photographer. Each order arrives with a written licence, a proper invoice and a numbered certificate — no intermediaries.

The admission standard behind all three is written up in the journal: the three tests every frame has to pass, and every field on an accession label.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Do you use AI?

No. Every photograph on this site is an original image captured with a physical camera. Nothing is generated, composited or enhanced by AI. The RAW files are archived and can be produced on request. That is the whole point of Beta Art.

How does "human verified" work?

Every photograph ships with its capture record — camera, lens, exposure, location, date. The original RAW file is kept in a permanent archive. If anyone questions whether a photograph is genuine, I can produce the RAW original, the EXIF data and, where the camera supports it, C2PA Content Credentials.

Where are the photographs taken?

Primarily in Norway and the Nordics, with some international work. Each photograph's location is stated on its catalogue entry.

Which licence do I need?

Personal — private projects, gifts, personal websites. Commercial — one business, on marketing, web, social and print up to 5,000 copies. Extended — unlimited print, products for resale, national campaigns. If you are unsure, email before you buy; I would rather help you pick the right tier than deal with it afterwards.

Can I edit or crop the photograph?

Yes, for design purposes — crop, colour adjustment, overlaid text. You may not use it as training data for AI, present it as your own work, or make it the primary sellable element of a product without an Extended licence.

Can I use the photograph for AI training?

No. Every licence explicitly excludes use in training generative-AI models, and that exclusion survives the licence term.

Is the licence exclusive?

No, unless you buy an exclusive licence. Standard licences are non-exclusive — the same photograph may be licensed to other buyers.

Can I transfer the licence to a client?

No. The licence is granted to the entity named on the invoice. Agencies buying on a client's behalf should use the Extended licence, which permits one client sublicence, or buy a separate licence per client.

How do I receive the file?

After payment you receive an email with a download link valid for 72 hours and a PDF licence certificate. The file is high-resolution sRGB JPEG, or TIFF on request.

Do I get an invoice?

Yes — a proper Norwegian invoice with organisation number and VAT where applicable, sent by email. You can generate a proforma yourself from the quote section above.

Is a model release on file?

Where a plate shows a recognisable person, a written model release covering the licensed uses is on file before that plate is offered. The release status is stated on the plate record. If a plate has no release, it is not sold for commercial use — no exceptions, and no case where you have to ask. The form itself is published in full: read the model release before you appear in a photograph, not after.

Can I commission a shoot?

Yes. Send the brief, the location, the date you need it by, and how the images will be used. You get a written scope and a fixed price before anything is booked. Commissioned work is licensed on the same three tiers, and the RAW originals are archived the same way.

Do you sell prints?

Not through the archive. What is sold here is a licence to a full-resolution digital file. If you want a physical print, ask — it is handled as a commission with the paper, size and finish agreed in writing, rather than as a product with a button.

How do I reach a person?

Email hallo@beta-art.com. Every licence, quote and dispute is handled by the photographer directly — there is no reseller, no agency and no support queue between you and the person who took the photograph.

Can I get a refund?

Because photographs are delivered digitally and you keep the file, refunds are limited to technical delivery failures, duplicate charges, and one thing more: if a plate sold to you as verified is ever shown to have been machine-generated, the licence fee comes back in full, with no time limit. That is the verification guarantee, and it is the only promise here that names its own consequence. See the licence terms for the rest.

What about the 14-day withdrawal right?

Under Norwegian law you have a 14-day right to withdraw digital purchases — unless you consent to receive the file immediately, in which case you waive that right. You are asked to confirm this at checkout.
