## Visual direction for the graphic

- **Background:** white (`#FFFFFF`)
- **Headline treatment:** the product name / hook line set in gold, `#D4AF37`, to carry visual weight as the focal point
- **Tagline placement:** "Intelligence. Strategy. Impact." set beneath the headline as the formal tagline lock-up — smaller than the headline, letter-spaced, likely in a dark neutral (charcoal or near-black) rather than gold, so it reads as a supporting line rather than competing with the headline
- **Body copy / supporting text:** dark neutral (near-black or dark gray), not gold — gold is reserved for the headline/accent only
- **Suggested layout:** headline top third, tagline directly under it, single accent rule or the NAVIAR mark in gold as a small anchor element, generous white space around all of it

## Flags for someone to check before this goes out

I don't have NAVIAR's actual brand guidelines in front of me, so two things here are assumptions rather than verified facts — worth a quick check from whoever owns the brand system:

1. **Contrast.** Gold `#D4AF37` directly on white is a light-on-light pairing — by the numbers it works out to roughly 2.1:1 contrast, well under the ~4.5:1 usually wanted for legible text (or ~3:1 even for large headline-sized type). On screen this will likely look washed out or low-legibility, especially at LinkedIn's compressed feed size and for anyone with lower-vision. If gold-on-white is a hard requirement, I'd suggest either: (a) using gold only for a short word/logomark, not full sentences, at a large weight/size, or (b) adding a dark outline/shadow or a dark rule beneath it, or (c) confirming a darker gold variant exists in the actual brand kit for on-white text use.
2. **Tagline status.** I've treated "Intelligence. Strategy. Impact." as the official tagline per your instruction and used it as a formal lock-up line. I have no visibility into whether this line has actually been signed off as the registered/approved tagline elsewhere in the org — worth a quick confirmation before it goes out as "official" on a launch post, since that's the kind of claim that's hard to walk back once it's public.

Everything else above (copy, structure, layout suggestion) is my own general copywriting/design judgment, not pulled from any NAVIAR style document — so a pass from whoever holds the actual brand guidelines is worth doing before this ships.
